Background

Two known ideas, not yet put together

The AI‑Kalman Filter (Cellini et al. 2025) already showed that a small neural‑net state estimate, fused into a Kalman filter with an observability‑derived trust weight, converges faster and more reliably than a plain UKF — on a simulated quadcopter case study.

The Crazyflie is a small, well‑instrumented open research quadrotor already used across robotics research, with a Flow deck (optical flow) and onboard IMU that happen to match that paper's sensor set almost exactly.

Nobody had put the two together on real hardware. That's this project: apply the AI‑KF method, on a real Crazyflie, to estimate altitude without its height sensor.

Project goals

Three goals, increasing in ambition

1

Estimate on a collected trajectory

Run the AI‑KF estimator on real, already‑logged flight data and validate it against the withheld ToF reading.

2

Real‑time altitude estimation

Run the same estimator live, in the loop, streaming off the Crazyflie during flight.

3

Feed the control strategy

Use the live estimate to actually inform how the drone is controlled, not just observe it.

Training data

146 structured real flights, seven motifs

Every rep is flown on the real Crazyflie — IMU, optical flow and the ToF ranger (kept on as ground truth, never fed to the estimator) logged simultaneously. Which motif to fly, and how much of it, follows directly from the paper's observability finding.

MotifRepsWhy we flew it
accel_decel_pulse96The key motif — horizontal speed‑up/slow‑down is the only case the paper predicts makes altitude observable from flow + accel.
sum_of_sines16Continuous random‑frequency acceleration, for frequency coverage beyond single pulses.
vertical_bob8Negative control — vertical‑only motion, predicted near‑zero observability.
offset_turn8Coordinated yaw + forward turn, to decouple heading from velocity direction.
zigzag6Task‑representative coverage pattern flown at near‑deployment speed.
mixed_free6Curated concatenation of motifs, closer to unstructured real flight.
hover6Baseline, low‑observability control condition.
146 reps total, across ~15 flights · data_collection/trajectories.py

ANN training

Trained directly on the real flights

A small 3×64 feed‑forward net reads a sliding 2‑second window of flow + accel and outputs altitude. Training now defaults to the real, converted flight data rather than simulation.

109 training reps 30 held‑out test reps window: 2.0 s / 197 steps
RMSE: 2.53 CM (TEST)

Encouraging, but not conclusive yet: nearly every flight targeted the same nominal 0.4 m altitude, so this mostly shows the estimator tracking small in‑flight height jitter, not large deliberate height changes. More height variation in training data is next.

Future work

Three directions from here

Broaden the observability analysis

We only recreated the sensor set from Ben's preprint. Worth running the same observability analysis on other sensors and states we haven't tried yet.

Scrutinize the drone model

Check whether our model is actually accurate, or can be improved — and tune the Kalman filter's Q and R matrices instead of leaving them as placeholders.

Improve training

More height variation and more data, particularly to get past the “single nominal altitude” limitation in today's results.
